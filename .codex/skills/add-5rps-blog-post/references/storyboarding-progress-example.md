# Storyboarding progress example

Use this pattern when a supplied session follows a film from screenplay notes into rough boards, tool research, and an animatic plan. Treat it as a structural example, not fixed copy.

## Input pattern

- `01_ideation/`: screenplay-derived visual notes, references selected manually by the director, location research, and setting or shot maps.
- `02_rough_storyboards/`: thumbnails, unrelated-work warm-ups, rough grayscale boards, rough cuts, and optional generated visualization frames.
- `03_research_attempt/`: second-by-second text prompts, generated test clips, re-edits, and isolated continuity failures.

## Story shape

1. Explain how director-curated references turn screenplay questions into visual notes.
2. Show how research into an actual location changed the setting and shot choices that had previously assumed an imagined space.
3. Move from thumbnails through warm-up drawings and rough boards. Label generated frames as visualization-only.
4. Describe text-only, second-by-second video prompting as an experiment. Keep source filenames in the internal session record, not in published captions. Use plain-language captions that identify the observed failure, and discuss anatomy, costume, character, prop, background, and spatial drift concretely.
5. State that a coherent sequence still requires artist judgment and foundational knowledge across drawing, staging, character design, background design, and continuity.
6. End with the next production step: complete the full-film grayscale boards, add temporary voices, build the CT/animatic, then revise timing, pacing, and cuts before Layout, Genga, Douga, and Shiage.

## Safety and rights

- Remove or obscure dialogue, internal paths, character names, sequence titles, explicit scene-purpose text, and other spoilers.
- Identify warm-ups from unrelated productions and require a rights check before deployment.
- Treat generated scenes as research visualizations, never final footage.
- When the director explicitly requests the original research audio in a local preview, keep it and place a clear bilingual copyrighted-music/research-use disclaimer beside the clips. Treat public deployment as a separate rights-review checkpoint. A disclaimer by itself does not establish clearance.
- Keep the website post as a local draft until the director approves publication.

## Approval-gated LinkedIn adaptation

After the director approves the blog, use the LinkedIn skill to write a 120–180 word company-page summary within its stated limit. Start with the production hook, place the canonical article URL on its own line immediately after that first paragraph, then continue with one practical lesson and the next step. End with the Discord invitation. Do not repeat or defer the article URL near the end.

Choose two to four upload images from the strongest non-generated evidence first: director-curated visual notes, the researched-location shot map, thumbnails, and rough grayscale boards. Avoid AI-generated frames and failure examples unless the director specifically approves them as necessary to the LinkedIn point. Stage the draft and images for manual approval. Do not publish automatically.
